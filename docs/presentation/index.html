1. What was migrated

The whole estate, not a representative subset. Every program, every copybook and every job member was read and accounted for; a single copybook with no inclusion anywhere was deliberately left behind, and that exclusion is recorded as a decision rather than an omission.

  • 28 COBOL programs — 17 online CICS transactions, 10 batch programs, 1 shared date-validation subprogram
  • 19,254 lines of COBOL source read as reference
  • 28 copybooks — data layouts, lookup tables, two procedural, one macro
  • 17 screen mapsets and their 17 generated symbolic maps
  • 29 job members plus 2 cataloged procedures
  • 544 procedure units, every one carried on a matrix row

The 544 is 528 paragraphs across the programs' procedure divisions plus 14 from the date-validation copybook and 2 from the attention-key copybook. Each has a row in the traceability matrix naming its source member, its source line, its target class, its target method and the test that covers it.

The legacy tree was never copied and never edited. The Java module is physically disjoint at carddemo-java/, and everything under app/ remains byte-identical to the analysed checkout — which is exactly what keeps the matrix verifiable against it. No legacy source text is reproduced in the module or in this deck for traceability purposes — not a program, not a paragraph, not a statement, and not in a comment. Traceability is by citation, never by transcription: member names, paragraph names, line numbers, record widths, byte offsets and key positions are interface metadata and appear freely; source lines do not appear at all. The claim is mechanically checkable rather than asserted — a grep over the module's Java comments for a COBOL or CICS verb carrying one of the legacy program's own identifiers, literals or argument lists returns nothing.

One category of legacy text does appear in the module, and naming it is the point of a certification. Where a preservation requirement obliges the module to emit legacy text byte for byte, that text is present as contract data rather than as a transcription: the seventeen fixed 80-column job-submission card images and their end-of-file sentinel in util/JclCardImageBuilder, the 80- and 100-byte statement template literals, and the seven sign-on message texts. Emitting those is what "external system interfaces MUST maintain identical contracts" requires — the card images are verified by draining them back out of a real queue — and it is a different act from carrying a paragraph's implementation across, which nothing here does.

The repository carries no Git tags, so the checkout commit is the only precise anchor on the source side of every mapping. The release stamp is carried in the trailer of 78 legacy members, which is how the estate identifies its own vintage.

The screen contract that became a machine contract

The mapsets are consumed as field-level contract authorities — names, lengths, numeric typing, required-field flags and the two-state error decoration behind the attribute bytes. They are not consumed as a visual design.

No browser interface is built, and that is a scope decision rather than an omission. The mandated target stack names no frontend technology, no component library and no design system, and the correct rendering of a terminal contract under a no-feature-expansion constraint is a machine contract. The screens therefore become request and response contracts exercised by tests, with the machine-readable interface description published by the module itself. Attention-key handling is centralised, including the legacy behaviour that the upper twelve function keys fold onto the same twelve actions as the lower twelve rather than being distinct.

3. The target module

One self-contained Maven module at carddemo-java/, carrying everything needed to build, run, migrate, observe and verify itself. A clean checkout needs a JDK and a container runtime and nothing else — no preinstalled build tool, no mainframe asset, no external procedure. The base package is com.carddemo, and the doubled d is deliberate: every package, every import and every configuration key sits beneath that exact spelling.

Every persistence and validation annotation is a jakarta import; the production tree carries no legacy-namespace import line at all and no wildcard import anywhere, which is what lets the unsafe-code audit be performed by inspection.

Where the 544 units actually live, because "twenty-six services own them all" is the tidy version and it is wrong in two directions. Counted from the target-class column of traceability-matrix.md, the rows resolve to twenty-two owning classes: twenty-one services, plus util.PfKeyTranslator, which owns the two paragraphs of the attention-key copybook and is a utility precisely because five online programs include that copybook rather than one owning it.

Five of the twenty-six translation-bearing services own no paragraph at all, and that is correct rather than a gap. The validation-lookup, navigation, message-catalogue, abend and job-submission services derive from copybooks and cross-cutting constructs rather than from procedure divisions — a lookup table, a dispatch graph, a message catalogue, an abend structure and a queue write are each data or a mechanism that paragraphs use, not paragraphs themselves. So saying twenty-six services own all 544 would credit five classes with rows they do not have and hide the one utility class that does own rows.

The batch tier is nine jobs, and the reason is a measurement

Most job steps in the estate do not run an application program at all: they define, delete, copy or toggle datasets. Only nine steps invoke an application program, which is why the module carries nine job configurations rather than one per step. Everything else is absorbed by the Flyway migrations, the Compose service definitions and the test fixtures. The step-level accounting is recorded in the decision log.

There is no master orchestrator class, and none is implied. Each job is independently launchable, and the order in which the jobs are normally run is an operational convention documented in architecture.md — not a coded dependency between jobs. One job is defined without any legacy driver at all, because the program it reproduces is complete but is invoked by no job member, procedure or resource definition. It is registered as launchable by name like the other eight and excluded from every sequence: nothing selects it implicitly, so it runs only when an operator or a test names it. Unsequenced is not unreachable, and the decision log records the distinction so the job is not read as dead configuration.

4. Faithful beats idiomatic

One tie-break decided every hard case: where faithful reproduction and idiomatic Java conflict, faithful wins, and the divergence is written down rather than resolved by taste. The decisions below are the ones that would otherwise have compiled cleanly, read plausibly and been wrong — and would have been invisible to a test written under the same misunderstanding.

Arithmetic truncates, because nothing in the estate rounds
The rounding keyword occurs zero times across every program and copybook in the estate. An arithmetic store without it truncates toward zero, so every monetary scale operation uses RoundingMode.DOWN. The conventional HALF_EVEN would differ by one cent on roughly half of all interest computations, and a single codec owns the policy so that no service can introduce a second one.
Expression order is reproduced operand for operand
Truncation makes arithmetic non-associative, so an algebraically identical rearrangement moves the truncation point and changes the result. Multiplication that precedes a division stays in that order. One report program contains no arithmetic at all, and none was introduced into it.
Zoned decimal, not packed decimal
No persisted monetary or rate field in the estate uses packed decimal, so no packed decoder is required. What is required is a codec for the overpunched sign convention, where the final byte carries both the low-order digit and the sign. Every monetary field becomes a BigDecimal whose scale matches its picture clause exactly; no field anywhere becomes a binary floating-point type.
The statement generator is a state machine, not a loop
That program is a hand-rolled dispatcher: a work field holds the name of the next phase, each phase sets it and jumps backward to the dispatcher, and the dispatcher re-branches on the new value. Nested method calls cannot reproduce re-entry into a dispatcher after a state change, so the service is an explicit state enum driven by a loop over a switch. Of 134 unconditional jumps estate-wide, 125 jump forward to an exit label and become a plain return; only 9 jump backward and form loops, and six of those nine belong to this one program.
Evaluation order is the contract
111 multi-way selections become switch constructs with clause order preserved, because the legacy evaluates top to bottom and stops at the first match — so reordering clauses changes behaviour wherever conditions overlap. The 508 condition names become enum constants with predicate methods, which converts flag-string comparison into type-checked state.
The queue bridge is adapted, not reinvented
The single online-to-batch bridge becomes a first-in-first-out queue publish that preserves all three contractual properties: 80-character fixed records, append ordering, and ignore-on-error semantics — so a publish failure logs and continues rather than aborting the caller, exactly as the legacy resource definition specifies.
Record mappers are hand-written, because the reflection budget is zero
All twelve mapper classes slice their record image by explicit offset arithmetic: one per eleven persisted record layouts, plus one for the statement job’s transient work record, which is written and re-read inside a single job and never stored. That is a design constraint rather than a preference: a zero reflection count rules out every annotation-driven mapping library, and it is also why no annotation processor appears anywhere in the dependency set.
One macro expansion becomes one method
A macro copybook carrying three substitution slots is expanded 39 times in a single program, against 39 distinct validation flags and field names. Honouring the directive means collapsing those expansions into 39 calls to one field-decoration method — the largest single de-duplication in the refactor — while keeping the two error states the macro distinguishes as separate states on the error contract, and keeping the behaviour that decoration fires only on re-submission.

Defects are reproduced, never propagated and never quietly corrected

  • An empty fee-computation paragraph is genuinely invoked and implements nothing. It survives as an explicitly documented no-op method. No fee logic was invented to fill it, because that would be feature expansion and would change what an interest run emits.
  • Two account-update fields carry no validation constraint in the Java contract, for two different reasons, and collapsing them would misrepresent both. The second address line is genuinely never validated by the legacy program — decorated for error display and nothing more — so attaching no constraint is faithful translation. The middle name is not: the legacy program does validate it, through its optional-alphabetic edit, and its not-OK flag is tested when the cursor is placed; the comment beside its decoration macro claims no edits are coded and is wrong about the code it sits next to. Java attaches no constraint regardless, because the migration direction said not to — so that one is a directive-driven divergence, recorded as an exception rather than presented as fidelity.
  • A commented-out alternative label for one menu option stays inactive, exactly as it is in the source.
  • An alphabetic check written as blank-the-letters-and-trim therefore passes embedded spaces. The faithful predicate is "every character is a letter or a space", not a per-character letter test, which would reject two-part names the legacy accepts.
  • An upper-casing step uses a strict 26-character substitution table, so it is reproduced as an ASCII-only fold rather than a locale-sensitive, Unicode-aware case conversion.
  • Identifier generation reads the highest existing key and adds one inside the same transaction. A database sequence would diverge permanently after the first rollback, so none is used.
  • Thirty-one source anomalies — misspelled field names, a duplicated paragraph label, a duplicated step name, a conflicting generation limit, a dangling program definition — are recorded in the anomaly register in the decision log. Fourteen of the thirty-one were named in the migration plan, together with a fifteenth observation; the remaining sixteen were found at the same checkout during translation. Where correcting one would alter a record layout or an external contract, it is preserved as-is and the defect is noted instead.

Two departures, both labelled as improvements rather than reproductions

The legacy compares a stored eight-character cleartext credential directly. The target stores BCrypt hashes only. This is the one place the migration knowingly does not reproduce legacy behaviour, and it is recorded as a deliberate, documented parity exception — reproducing the cleartext compare would have satisfied parity and violated the credential constraint. Beyond that, the production profile resolves every confidential configuration value from an environment variable with no fallback default, so a missing one fails startup instead of silently binding a placeholder, and the seeded identities used locally are hashes scoped to non-production profiles.

Every legacy file is defined with uncommitted reads, no recovery and no journalling, with correctness resting on record locking plus the programs' own before-and-after image comparison. The target runs PostgreSQL read-committed isolation with JPA versioning. Both changes are strictly stronger than the baseline, and saying so explicitly is what stops a reviewer reading the stronger posture as a behavioural regression.

What is deliberately not implemented

No feature was added. Nothing in the target does anything the estate did not already do — no new screen, field, report or job — and the boundary is drawn in four named places:

  • The illustrative product, fee and feature entities on the data-model diagram have no copybook, program or dataset behind them, so they are not implemented.
  • Three job members that enabled or disabled online access to a file, or drove the resource-definition utility, have no runtime equivalent once the keyed clusters are replaced. They are documented as intentionally unmigrated, not dropped.
  • One copybook has no inclusion anywhere in the estate. Migrating it would have created dead Java code, so it is recorded as a decision rather than an omission.
  • No browser interface and no application cache: the first is outside the named stack, and the second would change latency and consistency characteristics the estate never had.
  • Transport security needs a distinction, because one half of it is delivered. The application terminates TLS itself on the production profile — the listener is switched on there and it consumes externally supplied key-store material, resolved from the environment with no fallback, so a missing value fails start-up rather than quietly serving plaintext. What is not in scope is the infrastructure around it: no ingress or reverse proxy is provisioned, and no certificate is issued, renewed or rotated here. Saying "no transport termination" would understate the delivery; saying transport security is complete would overstate it. The application listens over TLS when configured to; the surrounding estate is somebody else's provisioning step.

5. How it is verified

Eight acceptance criteria, every one of them executable locally. None requires a production environment, a staging environment or a running mainframe: each runs against the Compose stack or against a container the test itself starts. This slide states what each criterion verifies; gate-evidence.md is the single authority for what any given run recorded, and it names the command and the artefact behind every result.

The eight acceptance criteria and the obligation each one discharges.
#CriterionWhat it verifies
1End-to-end boundary that a production-representative input is processed end to end locally and the emitted records are byte-equivalent to committed golden files at all five compared widths, which are the five contractual widths — 40, 80, 100, 133 and 430 bytes. Four are compared from a single seeded pipeline pass; the 40-byte category-balance report line is compared from a dedicated run of the job that emits it, because that job is the one emitter the primary pipeline does not drive. The comparison is byte-array equality on fixed-width records, so a trailing space or a sign overpunch fails rather than passing quietly. Mocked input and output does not satisfy it.
2Zero-warning build that a clean checkout compiles with every lint category enabled and warnings promoted to errors at release 25. The criterion is mechanical rather than reviewed: a warning is a build failure, not an entry in a report, so warnings cannot accumulate silently.
3Performance baseline that elapsed time, peak memory and records per second are measured inside the run that takes them and recorded with the fixture volumes they were measured over. Timers on every endpoint and every batch step corroborate the shape; the quoted figure comes from the run-scoped recorder rather than from a scrape. This criterion establishes the first Java baseline; it does not test a threshold — see the note below.
4Named validation artefacts that the nine ASCII fixtures and twelve encoded sequential datasets are named individually, present at their measured byte counts, and driven through the primary batch pipeline — together with the seeded identities and the five lookup cardinalities (490 phone area codes, being 410 general-purpose plus 80 easily-recognisable; 56 state codes; 240 state and postal-prefix combinations).
5Interface contract verification that each external interface is exercised against the real contract rather than self-certified: the fixed-width file formats, the operator-visible sign-on message texts and the routing the delivered user type decides, and the batch-trigger card image drained back out of a real queue rather than read off a builder's return value.
6Unsafe and low-level code audit that the counts of raw query-string assembly, process launching, reflection, unchecked casts and suppressed warnings are documented. The audit is scoped to the production Java tree, which matters: an unscoped scan would count the versioned schema migrations as raw query text and inflate the figure. The warning suppression is the single exception and is counted over both source trees, because criterion 2 forbids the construct outright and the annotation hides a warning wherever it is written.
7Scope matching that the extended tier is genuinely covered — multi-subsystem batch, file input and output, inter-program calls, job orchestration and cloud-service integration — behind an enforced, build-failing floor on line coverage.
8Integration sign-off that all seven of the above hold together, plus a dependency vulnerability scan whose exact recorded result is zero unsuppressed critical, zero unsuppressed high, one high finding covered by a written analyst determination (CVE-2026-66299, CVSS 7.5, matched on tomcat-embed-core, scoped to that identifier alone and self-expiring) and one visible below-threshold medium (CVE-2026-41178, CVSS 5.3, reported and not suppressed) — stated that way rather than as “no critical or high finding”, because a scanner finding that an analyst determined to be a false match is still a finding, and a deck that rounds it away is the reason a reader would stop checking. The dated report is published in Gate Evidence, and a CVE result ages, so the scan is re-run before any sign-off. Plus traceability covering 100% of the estate's procedure units.

Three conventions that keep the evidence honest

A measurement is never a threshold
No numeric service level of any kind exists anywhere in the estate — not in the COBOL, not in the job streams, not in the resource definitions. There is therefore nothing to compare against, which is exactly why the third criterion establishes a baseline instead of asserting one. This deck publishes no such figure at all, and any figure belonging to one run on one machine is recorded in gate-evidence.md beside the date and the machine, to be re-measured rather than quoted.
The coverage floor is on line coverage, and only line coverage is gated
That floor fails the build. Branch, method and instruction coverage are reported for information and are not thresholds — presenting a branch figure as one would misstate the criterion.
This deck states obligations; the evidence page states outcomes
No result, count, percentage or scan verdict is reproduced here. The separation is deliberate: a summary that restates a measured figure goes stale the moment the figure is re-measured, and a reader would then hold two answers with no way to tell which is current. There is one authority for outcomes, and it is gate-evidence.md.

On provenance, and on where the obligations came from

Two identifiers are the durable link between this module and the estate it came from, and they are the only correct answer to "which COBOL did this Java come from?": the analysed checkout commit 7756d895ffeb65f7ea72aaa609e356d9899afcec and the upstream release stamp CardDemo_v1.0-15-g27d6c6f-68 of 2022-07-19. Both appear in the header of the traceability matrix and of the decision log.

Two bodies of binding content are easy to misattribute, so they are named for what they are. The construct pairing on slide 2 is a requirement of the refactor. The eight criteria on slide 5 are acceptance criteria, and their evidence is published on gate-evidence.md. Separately, no user-specified rules were provided for this engagement — a verified absence rather than an unread document. That is not licence to lower the standard: the work is held to enterprise-standard best practice instead, as a conscious position, and the two standards binding this page in particular are licence continuity, which is why the Apache-2.0 header opens the file, and the prohibition on credential values in source, which is why no credential, password, secret, token or key value appears anywhere on it — not a real one, and not a placeholder shaped like one. Identifiers are the opposite case and appear freely: the checkout commit, the release stamp, the module coordinate, transaction and program names, paragraph names, record widths and byte offsets are all metadata, and naming them is precisely what makes a citation checkable against the estate.
